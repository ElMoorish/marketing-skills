# Campaign review checklist

Detail for each pass in `SKILL.md`. Not legal advice: rules vary by country and change.
When a check is high-risk, the right recommendation is "get legal review".

## 1. Message and offer

- The headline says what the reader gets, in their words, not the company's.
- One primary call to action. Secondary actions are visually quieter.
- The promise in the ad matches the headline on the landing page (message match).
- Price, discount, deadline and terms are identical everywhere they appear.
- The reader can tell why to act now without fake urgency.

## 2. Claims and proof

| Claim type | Needs |
|---|---|
| Numbers ("saves 10 hours") | A source in the approved proof list, plus any qualifier |
| Comparisons ("2x faster than X") | Current, documented, fair comparison |
| Superlatives ("best", "#1", "leading") | Independent evidence, or soften to a specific, true claim |
| Testimonials and reviews | Real customer, permission, and disclosure if they were paid or given anything |
| Customer logos | Written permission |
| Guarantees ("guaranteed results") | Real terms the business honors, stated clearly |
| "Free" | No hidden required purchase; trial terms and auto-renewal disclosed |

Anything not in the approved proof list: mark `Unsupported` and suggest either a source
to add or a rewrite that drops the claim.

## 3. Compliance risk flags

- **Endorsements and influencers:** clear disclosure of paid or gifted relationships
  (e.g. the US FTC Endorsement Guides, UK ASA/CAP code).
- **Email:** sender identified, physical address where required, working unsubscribe,
  consent basis for the list (e.g. CAN-SPAM in the US, GDPR/PECR in the UK/EU, CASL in Canada).
- **SMS:** prior express consent, opt-out instructions.
- **Privacy:** tracking pixels and cookies disclosed; consent banners where required.
- **Pricing and subscriptions:** total price clear, auto-renewal and cancellation terms disclosed.
- **Regulated categories:** health, financial, legal, alcohol, gambling, crypto, products
  for children, and political ads have extra platform and legal rules. Flag for legal review.
- **Platform ad policies:** check the specific ad platform's policies for the category.
- **Accessibility:** alt text, readable contrast, captions on video.

## 4. Brand and voice

- Compare against the writing samples in brand context section 8.
- Search for banned words from sections 5 and 8.
- Generic AI phrasing to cut: throat-clearing intros, "In today's...", "Let's dive in",
  "It's not just X, it's Y", "unlock/elevate/supercharge", "game-changer", stacked
  adjectives, tidy moralizing endings, and lists of exactly three by habit.
- Product names, capitalization and spelling follow the brand rules.

## 5. Audience fit

- Pain points match section 4 of brand context.
- Nothing that would attract the anti-personas from section 2.
- Paid targeting: location, language, age and interests consistent with the customer profile;
  exclusions set for existing customers if the goal is acquisition.

## 6. Tracking and mechanics

- Every link opens the right page (no 404s, no staging URLs).
- UTM naming is consistent: `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign` (+ `utm_content` for variants).
- The conversion event fires on the thank-you state, not the form view.
- Landing page loads fast on mobile and the form works end to end.
- Budget, schedule, time zone and end date are set as intended (for paid campaigns).
- A plan for when to check results and what number means "working".
